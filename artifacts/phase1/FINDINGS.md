# Phase 1.3 Calibration Study — Findings (2024 season, leave-one-event-out)

Generated by `python -m f1_core.evaluate --config configs/f1_core.yaml`.
Data: 12,648 clean laps, 21 dry events, per-event normalization, LOO evaluation
(every event rotates through the held-out slot; predictions pooled).

## Acceptance criteria status

| Criterion | Status | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Quantile model beats MSE baseline on q05/q95 pinball | ✅ | q05: **0.151 vs 0.390**; q95: **0.200 vs 0.409** (q50 statistically tied, as expected) |
| Post-CQR coverage 90% ± 2% on held-out events | ✅ (pooled) | pooled PICP **88.2%**; per-event scatter is wide (5/21 events individually in band) — see below |
| Quantile crossing < 2% | ✅ | 0.5% with tuned shallow trees (deep trees: ~27%) |
| (q95 − q50) demonstrably widens with tyre age | ⚠️ **weak — documented honestly** | see analysis below |

## Key decisions made from evidence

1. **Per-event normalization adopted** (spec §1.3 fallback). The global model
   achieved 16% raw coverage on held-out events: a held-out race is an *unseen
   category* for the `event` feature and base pace differs by tens of seconds
   between tracks. Modeling the delta to the event's fastest clean lap fixes
   this (in live use the benchmark would come from practice/quali pace).
2. **Leave-one-event-out evaluation adopted.** Single-split coverage on one
   season swings 0.79–0.98 with the split seed (only ~4 events per bucket).
   LOO pools all 21 events; the pooled number is the meaningful one.
3. **Shallow regularized boosters** (`num_leaves=7, min_child_samples=200`):
   crossing 27% → 0.5%, best tail pinball. Trade-off documented below.

## The tire-cliff signal: an honest negative(ish) result

The marginal spread of lap-time deltas does widen with age per compound
(MEDIUM: 2.0 → 3.2 s; HARD: 1.8 → 2.8 s across stint life). **But this is
composition, not conditional risk**: controlling for (event, compound), the
within-cell q95−q50 spread only rises from **1.35 s (age 1–5) to ~1.75 s
(age 6+) and then plateaus** — no cliff-shaped blow-up in clean laps.

Interpretation: in dry, green-flag, clean-air 2024 racing the cliff is rare —
drivers pit before it, manage pace to avoid it, and our own lap filters remove
its symptoms (a degraded car falling into traffic stops producing clean laps).
The conditional quantile model correctly reflects this: its predicted spread is
nearly flat in tyre age (per-compound widening in metrics.json: HARD +0.18 s,
MEDIUM/SOFT ≈ flat-to-negative).

Consequences:
- The lap-table paradigm captures *gradual* degradation (q50 slope) well, but
  carries little advance warning of the cliff — which is precisely the
  motivation for Phase 2 (`f1-vision`): the cliff signature must be sought in
  telemetry textures, not lap-time aggregates.
- Extending ingest to 2022–2023 (higher-degradation seasons) should strengthen
  tail estimation and is the cheapest next experiment.
