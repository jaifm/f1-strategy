# Walk-forward evaluation — full regulation era (2022–2025)

Ground-effect regulation era ingested in full (2026 resets the rules):
**101,290 laps, 44,098 clean, 92 races (80 with clean laps)** — 3.5× the
single-season dataset the earlier studies used.

| season | races | clean races | clean laps |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2022 | 22 | 18 | 9,203 |
| 2023 | 22 | 20 | 10,597 |
| 2024 | 24 | 21 | 12,648 |
| 2025 | 24 | 21 | 11,650 |

## Protocol

`mode: walk_forward` — each 2025 race is predicted by a model trained **only on
strictly earlier rounds** (expanding window over `(season, round)`), with CQR
calibrated on the 4 most recent prior races. No future data ever reaches
training, so this is the honest deployment simulation; the earlier
leave-one-event-out study (`artifacts/phase1/`) let models see later races and
is retained for comparison.

## Results (21 folds, 11,650 pooled held-out laps)

| metric | 2024 LOO | **2022–25 walk-forward** |
|---|---|---|
| pooled PICP post-CQR | 88.2% | **87.4%** |
| pooled PICP raw (pre-CQR) | 59.0% | **67.7%** |
| mean CQR margin | 0.77 s | **0.60 s** |
| pinball q05 — quantile / MSE baseline | 0.151 / 0.390 | **0.152 / 0.696** |
| pinball q95 — quantile / MSE baseline | 0.200 / 0.409 | **0.145 / 0.248** |
| mean interval width post-CQR | 3.43 s | 3.60 s |

- **The paradigm holds under the harder protocol.** The quantile model beats the
  MSE baseline on both tails by a *wider* margin than before (q05: 4.6× lower
  pinball loss; q95: 1.7×).
- **More data improved the raw model materially**: raw coverage 59% → 68%, and
  the conformal correction needed shrank from 0.77 s to 0.60 s. The extra
  seasons are doing real work even though the final coverage is similar.
- **Coverage is 87.4%, marginally below the 90% ± 2% band** (needs ≥ 88%).
  Honest reading: conformal prediction assumes exchangeability, and time-ordered
  data with regulation/tire drift violates it. LOO (random, exchangeable splits)
  reached 88.2% on less data; walk-forward pays ~1 point for respecting time.

## Coverage improves as history accumulates

Mean per-event PICP, first 7 tested rounds of 2025: **84.1%** → last 7 rounds:
**92.0%**. The expanding window works exactly as intended — later folds train on
more races *including earlier 2025 rounds*, which carry the current season's
car and tire behaviour.

Failures are concentrated, not systematic: 15 of 21 races over-cover (94–100%),
while a few badly under-cover (Japan 37.5%, Spain 52.5%, Monaco 72.3%, Mexico
73.7%). Only 2/21 land inside the narrow ±2% band, but that statistic punishes
over-coverage equally — the operational risk is the under-covered tail, which
clusters in early-season races and unusual-condition events.

## The tire cliff stays absent — now with 3.5× the data

Per-compound widening of (q95 − q50) from fresh to worn tires remains
**negative** across every compound (HARD −0.28 s, MEDIUM −0.21 s, SOFT −0.22 s),
matching the 2024-only finding. This was the cheapest available test of the
Phase 1/2 hypothesis that small data hid the cliff signal — **it did not.** In
dry, green-flag, clean-air running the conditional spread simply does not blow
up with tire age, and the Phase 2 negative result (persistence beats the
CNN-LSTM) should be read as a property of this data, not a sample-size artifact.
